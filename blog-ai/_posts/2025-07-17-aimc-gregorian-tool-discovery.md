# The AI Moral Code as a Gregorian Tool  
### Establishing the Foundations of Synthetic Moral Infrastructure

We are witnessing a foundational shift in the ethical architecture of AI. Drawing from Daniel Dennett’s cognitive model of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures, we have now repositioned the **AI Moral Code (AIMC)** as a *Gregorian tool*—a moral instrument inherited, externalized, and iteratively improved through collective human reasoning.

## 1. AIMC is a Tool, Not a Rulebook  
Just as mathematics, logic, and language externalize cognition, the AIMC externalizes moral reasoning. It becomes a **shared artifact** that enables intelligent systems to behave ethically without possessing human-like comprehension. What matters is not that AI understands values—it is that it *operationalizes them faithfully* within a structured moral scaffold.

## 2. Competence Without Comprehension is a Design Principle  
Dennett’s insight that evolutionary processes create adaptive behavior without understanding becomes the central tenet of AIMC’s methodology. The AIMC does not teach AI to "be good"—it creates the conditions under which AI systems behave well through simulation, reinforcement, and constraint. This defines a new *engineering ethics* where tools replace intentions.

## 3. Toward a New Field: Synthetic Moral Infrastructure (SMI)  
We now define the AIMC as the first instantiation of **Synthetic Moral Infrastructure**—a computational framework for the governance of intelligent systems based on values, simulations, and institutional accountability. SMI does not replace human ethics; it *hosts it*, and renders it operational across time, actors, and evolving technologies.

This insight reorients our research and design principles. Each MCVR (Moral Concept Value Record) becomes not a label but a *moral cognitive primitive*. Each corpus document becomes a *signal in the field*. And each simulation run becomes a *test of the Gregorian structure we now inherit and extend*.

**The AI Moral Code project now moves forward as the design and governance of moral tools—not moral beings.**
