## Simulation Methodology

Ethics that cannot be tested cannot be trusted. The AI Moral Code employs simulation as a moral calculus—transforming abstract values into actionable choices within constrained environments. Simulation enables us to observe, evaluate, and recalibrate ethical behavior in complex AI-human systems.

### Why Simulation?

Simulation is not speculation. It is operational ethics. This methodology enables:

- Constraint validation (Normative layer)  
- Conflict arbitration (Behavioral layer)  
- Jurisdictional enforcement (Regulatory layer)  
- Foresight modeling (Conceptual layer)

It models real-world moral dilemmas in controlled environments, using AI agents, value activation triggers, and audit-driven outcomes.

### The NRBC Framework in Action

Each simulation is governed by the NRBC architecture:

- **Normative**: Non-negotiable values such as justice, dignity, and responsibility guide allowable bounds.  
- **Regulatory**: Domain-specific laws and sectoral policies act as runtime constraints (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA).  
- **Behavioral**: Outcomes are scored by traceability, fairness distribution, and trust generation.  
- **Conceptual**: Value clusters are tested for semantic coherence and translatability across sectors.

### Ethical Sprint Model

Simulations unfold in ethical sprints, each focused on a moral testbed:

1. **Fairness Sprint** – Measures distributional impacts across stakeholders.  
2. **Humility Sprint** – Introduces uncertainty thresholds and model introspection.  
3. **Conflict Sprint** – Tests tradeoffs among values (e.g., privacy vs. accountability).

Each sprint logs outputs into a Moral Ledger, preserving justifications, conflicts, and revisions for future arbitration.

### Value Activation Engine

Simulations activate value nodes through:

- Role-based actions (human vs. AI vs. hybrid)  
- Domain triggers (healthcare, defense, education, etc.)  
- Risk tiers and scenario complexity  
- Pre-defined dilemmas with moral branching logic

### Example Simulation

**Dilemma**: An autonomous vehicle must decide between swerving to avoid a jaywalker (beneficence) or staying its course to protect its passengers (responsibility, fairness).

Simulation output:
- Trust index: 63%  
- Conflict node: dignity vs. safety  
- Ethical Arbitration Output: escalate to human-in-the-loop  
- Drift flag: privacy weight declined by 17% under risk amplification

### Metrics of Moral Performance

- **Ethical Alignment Score (EAS)**  
- **Conflict Resolution Trace (CRT)**  
- **Salience Shift Delta (SSD)**  
- **Agent Justification Tree (AJT)**  

These outputs inform revisions to both value weightings and scenario parameters.

### Toward an Ethical Design Loop

All simulations form a feedback loop into:

- The Composite Value Score (CVS) system  
- Ethical Salience Tracker (EST)  
- Policy update proposals for real-world alignment

Simulations are not passive—they encode the living ethical infrastructure of the Code.
